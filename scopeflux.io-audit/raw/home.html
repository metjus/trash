Expired domains, explained.

How expiring, pending delete and dropped domains work, and how to get one when it is released.

What are expired domains?

An expired domain is a name whose owner did not renew it by its domain expiration date. It is not free yet: the owner usually still has a grace period and a redemption period. Only when the registry deletes it, when the domain drops, can anyone register it.

What does pending delete mean?

Pending delete is the last stage before a domain is deleted, usually five days for .com and .net. Nobody can renew the name any more. ScopeFlux lists pending delete and deleted domains with the day they are expected to drop.

How can I buy an expired domain?

Wait for the release day and register the name at any registrar, or place a backorder with a drop catching service before it drops. Popular names are often caught within seconds. ScopeFlux does not sell or register domains; it shows when a name is expected to become available.

When does a domain expire, and when is it released?

Use the domain expiration checker to see a domain’s expiration date from public registry data. For listed names, ScopeFlux shows the estimated release day from the source: Verisign pending-delete lists for .com and .net, the registries’ own drop lists for .it and .au, Backorder.sk for .sk and CZ.NIC for .cz.

Which extensions are covered?

.com, .net, .org, .io, .app, .xyz, .info, .me, .biz, .us, .pro, .cc, .tv, .co.uk, .uk, .org.uk, .com.au, .au, .it, .sk and .cz. Expiring .sk and .cz domains are rarely covered by other expired domain lists.

Is the expired domains list free?

Yes. Browsing, search, filters and the watchlist are free. Signing in with Google is optional and keeps your watchlist on all your devices. CSV export and API access are part of ScopeFlux Premium, a monthly plan.
